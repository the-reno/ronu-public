FINAL CFO PRESENTATION WORDING
==============================

Purpose
-------
Use one direct conclusion per slide. Keep the analytical chain explicit:
historical evidence -> directional reversal -> define risk -> robust allocation -> execution.

SLIDE 1 — HISTORICAL EVIDENCE
-----------------------------
Headline:
Short tenors delivered similar realized returns; 1M edged ON while 6M lagged

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S1_Historical!A2.
With the current full sample, 1M exceeds ON by only ~0.5 bp while 6M lags ON by ~5.3 bp over the average common 6M horizon.

Chart wording:
Historical overnight rate path
Historical excess return vs ON
Y/X value axis: Excess return vs ON (bp)

Speaker wording:
"Starting with what actually happened, short tenors delivered very similar realized returns. 1M was marginally ahead of ON, while 6M lagged. The important point is not the 1M winner itself; it is that the realized path rewarded the ability to reprice rather than lock too early."

Presentation note:
Use excess return versus ON for the tenor comparison. Do not use a 0%-based absolute return chart because it visually hides the economically relevant bp differences.

SLIDE 2 — DIRECTION MATTERS
---------------------------
Headline:
The preferred tenor reverses when the rate path reverses

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S2_Direction!A2.
With the current full sample: historical winner = 1M at ~+0.5 bp vs ON; inverted winner = 6M at ~+17.6 bp vs ON.

Chart wording:
Excess return vs ON by rate direction
Value axis: Excess return vs ON (bp)

Speaker wording:
"When the direction of rates is reversed, the advantage moves toward longer tenor. Short cash benefits from repricing higher; 6M benefits from locking before rates fall. The decision therefore cannot be based on the historical winner alone."

Important:
ON is the zero reference and does not need to be plotted as a bar. Use a symmetric axis around zero. Historical and inverted paths are stress tests, not forecasts.

SLIDE 3 — DEFINE THE RISK
-------------------------
Headline:
The relevant risk is the cost of choosing the wrong tenor

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S3_Regret!A2.
With the current full sample: 100% ON regret is ~1.9 bp historical / ~17.7 bp inverted; 100% 6M regret is ~7.2 bp historical / ~0.1 bp inverted.

Chart wording:
Average cost of being wrong - lower is better
Value axis: Average regret (bp)

Speaker wording:
"The CFO does not need to forecast the path perfectly. The risk to control is the penalty from selecting the wrong tenor. We measure that penalty as regret: the difference between what the selected strategy earned and what the best tenor would have earned with perfect hindsight."

Interpretation:
ON protects the historical / higher-rate direction.
6M protects the falling-rate direction.
Neither protects both.

SLIDE 4 — ROBUST ALLOCATION
---------------------------
Headline:
The robust mix equalizes the cost of being wrong at ~5.5 bp

Core CFO message:
ON protects against higher-for-longer / rising rates; 6M protects against falling rates; the allocation is chosen to minimize the cost of being wrong in either direction.

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S4_Robust!A2.
With the current full sample, the robust allocation is 10% ON / 20% 1M / 70% 6M, with ~5.5 bp historical and ~5.5 bp inverted average regret.

Chart wording:
Directional regret by allocation - lower-left is better
X-axis: Historical-path average regret (bp)
Y-axis: Inverted-path average regret (bp)

Speaker wording:
"Each point is one long-only allocation across ON, 1M, 3M and 6M in 10% increments. We choose the portfolio that minimizes the larger average regret across the two directional stress paths. The objective is not to maximize return under one forecast; it is to keep the cost of being wrong small in either direction."

Interpretation:
- Lower-left is better.
- 100% ON protects the historical/rising side.
- 100% 6M protects the falling-rate side.
- The robust point is selected by the minimax rule and nearly equalizes the two directional regrets.
- Historical and inverted paths are stress tests, not forecasts.

FINAL MESSAGE
-------------
"The objective is not to maximize return under one interest-rate forecast. It is to preserve an attractive return while limiting the cost of being wrong about the direction of rates, and then execute that allocation consistently."
