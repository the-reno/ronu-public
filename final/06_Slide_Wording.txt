==============================

Purpose
-------
Use one direct conclusion per slide. Keep the analytical chain explicit:
historical evidence -> directional reversal -> define risk -> robust allocation -> execution.

SLIDE 2 — DIRECTION MATTERS
---------------------------
Headline:
The preferred tenor reverses when the rate path reverses

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S2_Direction!A2.
With the current full sample: historical winner = 1M at ~+0.5 bp vs ON; inverted winner = 6M at ~+17.6 bp vs ON.

Chart wording:
Excess return vs ON by rate direction
Value axis: Excess return vs ON (bp)

Speaker wording:
"When the direction of rates is reversed, the advantage moves toward longer tenor. Short cash benefits from repricing higher; 6M benefits from locking before rates fall. The decision therefore cannot be based on the historical winner alone."

Important:
ON is the zero reference and does not need to be plotted as a bar. Use a symmetric axis around zero. Historical and inverted paths are stress tests, not forecasts.

SLIDE 3 — DEFINE THE RISK
-------------------------
Headline:
The relevant risk is the cost of choosing the wrong tenor

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S3_Regret!A2.
With the current full sample: 100% ON regret is ~1.9 bp historical / ~17.7 bp inverted; 100% 6M regret is ~7.2 bp historical / ~0.1 bp inverted.

Chart wording:
Average cost of being wrong - lower is better
Value axis: Average regret (bp)

Speaker wording:
"The CFO does not need to forecast the path perfectly. The risk to control is the penalty from selecting the wrong tenor. We measure that penalty as regret: the difference between what the selected strategy earned and what the best tenor would have earned with perfect hindsight."

Interpretation:
ON protects the historical / higher-rate direction.
6M protects the falling-rate direction.
Neither protects both.

SLIDE 4 — ROBUST ALLOCATION
---------------------------
Headline:
Robust protection is anchored at 70% 6M; the short bucket remains flexible

Framework shown on slide:
Fix the 6M share at 0%, 10%, ... 100%. For each fixed 6M weight, choose the ON / 1M / 3M combination that minimizes the larger average regret across the Historical and Inverted rate paths. Then compare the resulting directional trade-off.

Direct conclusion:
Use the dynamic conclusion written by the VBA in S4_Robust!A2.
With the current full sample, the global minimax allocation is 10% ON / 20% 1M / 70% 6M. Historical average regret is ~5.52 bp and inverted average regret is ~5.48 bp, so worst directional regret is ~5.52 bp.

Chart A:
Directional regret as 6M allocation changes
X-axis: 6M allocation
Y-axis: Average regret (bp)
Message: increasing 6M protection reduces regret in the falling-rate stress but increases regret in the historical / higher-rate environment. The two risks converge around 70% 6M.

Chart B:
Worst directional regret is minimized near the crossover
X-axis: 6M allocation
Y-axis: Worst directional average regret (bp)
Message: the minimax protection curve bottoms around 70% 6M.

Chart C:
Near-optimal allocation region
Current sample: 9 portfolios remain within +0.25 bp of the optimum. All retain 70% in 6M, while the remaining 30% can be distributed across ON / 1M / 3M with little change in worst directional protection.
Portfolio labels are shown as ON / 1M / 3M / 6M.

Speaker wording:
"The first chart makes the trade-off explicit. As we add 6M, protection improves if rates fall, but the cost increases if rates remain high or rise. For every 6M weight I let the short bucket choose its best mix across ON, 1M and 3M. The two directional risks converge around 70% in 6M, which is also where the maximum of the two regrets is minimized. Importantly, this is not one fragile point: nine portfolios sit within a quarter basis point of the optimum, and all preserve the same 70% 6M anchor. That gives us flexibility to manage the remaining 30% based on liquidity needs and relative pricing."

Core CFO message:
The 70% 6M allocation is the stable duration anchor. The remaining short bucket can be managed tactically without materially changing directional protection.

Historical and inverted paths are stress tests, not forecasts.
